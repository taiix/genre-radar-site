/ why

I got tired of checking Steam by hand.

Picking a launch date is mostly guessing. You scroll the upcoming list, check the calendar, and hope nothing big lands the same week.

So I automated the boring part. A script reads Steam's store every night, keeps the history, and writes it up once a week. I use it for my own games.

/ the fine print

What it is. What it isn't.

It is

  • A weekly summary of public Steam store data.
  • Good for one question: what's happening in my genre right now?
  • Cheap. It runs without servers, so it stays cheap.

It isn't

  • Wishlist counts. Steam doesn't publish them. You get the rank.
  • Accurate sales. Ranges are reviews × 30–60. A size class, not a number.
  • Every upcoming game. It sees the top 100 most-wishlisted per tag.
  • Instant. New tags need a week for "+7 days" and 5 weeks for averages.

Questions

Which tags can I track?

Any of Steam's 429 user tags, spelled the way Steam spells them. The form suggests them as you type.

When does it arrive?

Mondays, 07:00 UTC. Data is refreshed every night, so it covers the full week before.

How is this different from SteamDB, Gamalytic or GameDiscoverCo?

Those go much deeper. Gamalytic has wishlist and revenue estimates, GameDiscoverCo Plus has analysis and a big data back end, and they cost $15–75 a month. Genre Radar does one narrow thing, weekly, for your tags, for about the price of a coffee.

How accurate are the sales numbers?

Not very. It's the "reviews × 30–60" rule of thumb, labelled as such. Cheap games, free weekends and key giveaways break it.

Can I change tags or my launch date?

Yes. Reply to any email or write to contact@stanmakes.games. Changes apply from the next Monday.
